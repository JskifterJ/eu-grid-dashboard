    ∑ How the Compute Siting Score works ▸

    CSS is a 0–100 score per country per hour. It combines four dimensions, weighted by a user-selectable preset. Each dimension is min-max normalized across all 35 countries for the same hour — so 100 is "best in Europe right now," not an absolute.

    1. Carbon intensity

    ENTSO-E generation mix × IPCC AR5 lifecycle emission factors.

    Grams of CO₂ per kWh of electricity delivered. Lower is better. IPCC median values: wind 11, nuclear 12, hydro 24, solar 41, gas 490, coal 820, lignite 1054, oil shale 1100.

    2. Cost (carbon-internalized)

    ENTSO-E day-ahead auction €/MWh + EU ETS carbon adder.

    Market €/MWh + (CO₂_g/kWh × €75/tCO₂ ÷ 1000). At €75/tCO₂ (2026 EU ETS spot), a 600 g/kWh grid adds €45/MWh. Internalizes the social cost of carbon into the economic signal.

    3. Renewable share

    ENTSO-E generation by source.

    % of current output from wind, solar, hydro, geothermal. Directional signal separate from carbon: a nuclear-heavy grid scores low here but high on carbon, and that tension is real.

    4. Price stability

    24h-rolling σ of ENTSO-E day-ahead prices.

    Intra-day standard deviation of €/MWh. Lower is better — a volatile grid makes capex-intensive GPU scheduling harder.

    The formula

    CSS(country) = 0.4·carbon_n + 0.3·cost_n + 0.2·renewable_n + 0.1·stability_n

    Weights shown are the Balanced preset. Green shifts to 60/15/20/5. Cost shifts to 10/60/15/15. Workload presets (training/fine-tuning/inference) use their own default weights — see the i icon beside the workload switcher.

    Inference latency penalty

    For inference workloads, each country's CSS is attenuated by physical distance from the chosen serving region's population centroid:

    penalty(c, region) = min(0.4, max(0, (dist_km(c, region) − 500) / 2500)) CSS_inference(c) = CSS(c) × (1 − penalty(c, region))

    500 km free zone ≈ real EU CDN round-trip tolerance. Penalty tops out at 40% around 3000 km. This is the physics of real-time serving: a Stockholm site for Iberian users means every request eats ~50 ms of extra one-way latency — deadly for chat UIs.

    Forecast (24h-ahead)

    Price forecast: ENTSO-E day-ahead auction publishes every hour of tomorrow — this is the market's forecast.

    CO₂ forecast: ENTSO-E day-ahead generation-forecast-by-source × the same IPCC factors we use for the current hour.

    Eval benchmark: naive seasonal baseline (same-hour-same-weekday mean over last 4 weeks) is computed alongside to measure whether the market forecast actually beats a trivial baseline — surfaced as MAPE on the (upcoming) forecast-accuracy panel.

    Time-of-day optimization

    The simulator's 24h ribbon shows forecasted CO₂ intensity hour-by-hour. For a workload of N contiguous hours, we sweep every start-time window and pick:

    best_window = argmin_{i ∈ [0, 24-N]} mean(co2[i..i+N]) worst_window = argmax_{i ∈ [0, 24-N]} mean(co2[i..i+N])

    Savings: (worst_window_avg − best_window_avg) / worst_window_avg × 100. The kg-CO₂-avoided number is mw × hours × (worst_avg − best_avg) — what you'd save by deferring a 10 MW × 6h job from evening peak to overnight trough.

    This is why agents building up overnight batches is an interesting product pattern — you're not just saving cost, you're physically avoiding carbon emissions that would have been produced by a peaker plant. The deferred-workload card below the simulator models what a "night rate" inference tier could look like.

    Life-cycle assessment (LCA)

    The simulator's Full LCA toggle replaces the operational-only view with a proper lifecycle breakdown:

    total_kg = operational + embodied_hardware + embodied_datacenter

    Operational = MW × hours × (g CO₂/kWh) × PUE. PUE (Power Usage Effectiveness) is the ratio of total facility energy to IT energy — defaults: Nordic 1.10, Northern EU 1.20, Central EU 1.25, Southern EU 1.40, Island 1.45. Free cooling in Norway/Sweden/Iceland is why Nordic co-location is so efficient.

    Embodied hardware amortizes GPU manufacturing CO₂ over a 3-year service life (the neocloud / hyperscale depreciation standard). Per-unit medians: A100 900 kg, H100 1,300 kg, H200 1,400 kg, B200 1,900 kg, MI300X 1,500 kg, Groq LPU 600 kg — from NVIDIA ESG filings, Patterson et al. 2021, and industry LCA studies. GPUs-per-MW varies by chip TDP (H100 ≈ 1,430/MW; B200 ≈ 1,000/MW).

    Embodied datacenter covers building structure + HVAC + power distribution, amortized at 7 kg CO₂/kW/year — the LBNL/industry-median from the 5–10 kg range. This is small relative to operational and hardware, but non-zero for hyperscale footprints.

    With clean operational power (France ~40 g/kWh), embodied carbon becomes the *dominant* share — operational drops to ~50% of total. That flips the conventional "choose Nordic for clean power" wisdom: once the grid is clean enough, hardware manufacturing is where the carbon lives. Extending GPU service life from 3 to 5 years would halve embodied hardware CO₂ per workload.

    Known caveats

    • Multi-zone countries (DE_LU, NO_1..NO_5, SE_1..SE_4, IT_NORD..IT_SARD, DK_1/DK_2) are aggregated to country level by summing generation GW and generation-weight-averaging prices. Flows are queried on the primary zone only.
    • Bidding-zone coverage differs across countries — some Balkan/Baltic data may be sparse in live ENTSO-E; mock data fills gaps when the API key is absent.
    • IPCC AR5 (2014) emission factors are lifecycle medians; newer IPCC AR6 values exist but AR5 remains the canonical reference for cross-country comparison.
    • The PEF (primary energy factor) toggle on the generation mix is separate from CSS — CSS scores on electricity-delivered basis because that's what compute buys.
    • LCA embodied-carbon figures carry ±30% uncertainty. Official NVIDIA Scope-3 product-level data for the newer H100 / B200 generations is still sparse; we use published ESG figures + Patterson-style extrapolations. Estimates should be treated as order-of-magnitude.
    • Time-of-day optimization assumes the workload is contiguously interruptible. Real training jobs may need a specific wall-clock window (e.g. a 72h run over a weekend); the 24h window selection generalizes to longer horizons but requires a longer forecast we don't currently publish.

    The story that explains why this dashboard is possible.
